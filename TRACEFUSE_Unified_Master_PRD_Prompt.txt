Build TRACEFUSE: THE INTELLIGENCE FUSION LAB — a polished, deeply interactive, technically credible blockchain-forensics and OSINT investigation platform for a hackathon problem statement called “Ghost Attacks.” The scenario involves stolen crypto assets being split among wallets, routed through bridges, and potentially obscured by privacy infrastructure. PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

Every transaction leaves evidence. Every clue needs a source. Every inferred connection must explain why it exists.

TRACEFUSE’s differentiation is the combination of:
1. Evidence Fusion Graph connecting wallet/transaction activity with public-source intelligence.
2. ChronoReplay, a time-controlled replay of actual transaction activity.
3. Matrix Mode, a selectable directed transaction matrix and time-window analytics view.
4. Explainable and auditable quantitative rankings that separate observed facts from hypotheses.
5. Cross-chain candidate tracing backed by documented bridge events where possible, with uncertainty made explicit.
6. A memorable scrollytelling landing page that introduces the investigation problem and leads into the genuinely working application.
7. A persistent Investigation Casebook and evidence-rich downloadable reports.

PART 4 — COMPLETE FUNCTIONAL SCOPE
============================================================

A. INVESTIGATION INTAKE
- Accept a wallet address or transaction hash and a selected chain where relevant.
- Validate address/hash format and input type; resolve transaction hashes through the provider when supported.
- Provide configurable supported-chain selection, time window, hop depth, and branching/query limits.
- Explain unsupported chains, invalid input, empty data, rate limits, and configuration issues.
- Start a real backend investigation and expose its actual lifecycle.

B. REAL ON-CHAIN DATA ACQUISITION
- Implement genuine server-side provider adapters for the selected blockchain API(s).
- Retrieve actual native-asset and supported token transfers; add internal transactions/NFT information only where supported and justified.
- Support provider pagination, rate-limit handling, timeouts, retries/backoff, caching, deduplication, and partial-result warnings.
- Normalize chain ID, transaction hash, block number, timestamp, sender, recipient, asset/token metadata, raw amount, decimals, display amount, provider, retrieval time, and explorer URL.
- Use appropriate decimal-safe numeric representations; do not lose precision by converting arbitrary token units to binary floating-point values too early.
- Preserve retrieval provenance. Mark records as live, cached, or from a labelled historical case, including freshness timestamps.
- Do not imply that an ordinary transaction endpoint gives complete decoded contract semantics or proof of the ultimate beneficial owner.

C. WALLET GRAPH AND FUND TRACING
- Build a directed graph where wallet/account or contract entities are nodes and observed transfers are edges.
- Support forward/backward tracing, configurable hop depth and branching limits, connected-component views, edge direction, amount, timestamp, asset, and transaction drill-down.
- Provide pan, zoom, fit-to-screen, node selection, edge selection, layout options, search, show/hide controls, filters, and focus on selected wallet/transaction.
- Display selected-wallet metrics, transactions, labels, source evidence, and known limitations in a side panel or inspector.
- Provide usable large-graph controls; avoid fetching or rendering unbounded graph neighborhoods.
- Handle contracts, exchange wallets, routing wallets, repeated activity, self-transfers, and incomplete provider data without silently asserting common ownership.

D. QUANTITATIVE ANALYSIS ENGINE
Implement transparent, testable metrics where inputs are present:
- Hop distance from the seed.
- In-degree/out-degree and fan-in/fan-out.
- Transaction count, frequency, velocity, and activity over a selected interval.
- Amount distribution and justified amount-similarity measures.
- Temporal deltas and candidate coordinated sequences.
- Connected components and suitable clustering methods.
- Centrality or other graph metrics where meaningful.
- Weighted directed adjacency matrix; optional separate matrices for counts, amounts, and time windows.
- Feature explanation, units, data requirements, limitations, and handling for unavailable values.

Metrics must be calculated from normalized records and update when applicable filters change. Test the mathematics independently of the UI. Deduplicate records before calculating totals/counts. Do not use invented missing values to make a chart look complete. Document that splitting, high transaction velocity, common destinations, or graph proximity alone do not prove malicious intent or common wallet ownership.

E. CHRONOREPLAY — INTERACTIVE TRANSACTION TIMELINE
- Provide a real chronological timeline for fetched/case transactions.
- Include play/pause, seekable time slider, playback speed, selected time display, and time-window controls.
- Reveal actual recorded transfers in chronological order and update graph state/visibility and relevant figures according to the chosen time.
- Permit selecting a transfer to inspect the source record and transaction.
- Make time filtering and calculations real; playback cannot be an animation disconnected from the underlying data.
- Clearly communicate gaps, timestamp quality, and records with unavailable timestamps.

F. MATRIX MODE
- Provide a toggle between the graph and a genuine directed matrix/heatmap view.
- Use wallet addresses/entities on rows and columns.
- Support transfer count and transferred-value measures and adjustable time windows.
- Make color intensity interpretable with a legend and robust empty/zero states.
- Support selection, hover details, search, scrolling, and drill-down from any non-empty cell to its underlying transactions.
- Keep graph/matrix selections synchronized where meaningful.
- Use sparse matrix techniques or virtualization when necessary; show clear limits on very large datasets.

G. OSINT COLLECTOR AND INTELLIGENCE ENRICHMENT
- Implement a real GitHub REST API adapter for permitted public repository/code-search use, respecting authentication and documented quotas.
- Run targeted searches for relevant wallet addresses, transaction hashes, exploit identifiers, and contextual terms rather than issuing uncontrolled broad searches.
- Extract and normalize wallet addresses and other supported indicators from accessible results.
- Preserve exact source URLs, repository/file identifiers, snippets, publication/commit timestamps when available, collection time, query context, and indicator match location.
- Integrate GoPlus address intelligence only for supported inputs and interpret each field according to the provider's documentation.
- Ingest the official OFAC sanctions dataset through a reproducible importer; track its source, retrieval date, and dataset version/hash as suitable.
- Evaluate additional accessible public threat-intelligence datasets/APIs where they strengthen the system; do not make essential workflows depend on a service with insufficient quota or unavailable partner-only access.
- Normalize results in a common Indicator/Evidence structure, cache them, deduplicate copied/repeated evidence, respect rate limits, and provide source health/freshness state.
- Distinguish address mention, source-reported abuse label, sanctions match, known malicious code, and identity evidence. They are not interchangeable.
- Preserve enough surrounding context to make a match reviewable. A GitHub mention is not proof of wallet ownership or culpability.
- Do not bypass access controls, harvest private communities, or include credentials or sensitive/private personal data.

H. EVIDENCE FUSION GRAPH — CENTRAL DIFFERENTIATOR
- Support typed entities such as wallets, transactions, repositories, public reports, intelligence indicators, labels/entities, and bridge events where applicable.
- Support typed relationships including observed transfer, documented bridge event, exact public-source match, source-reported label, heuristic candidate connection, and analyst-created note/relationship.
- Visually distinguish verified on-chain facts, external-source assertions, algorithmic inferences, and unverified hypotheses.
- On selecting any entity or relationship, show the exact evidence, URL, transaction hash, timestamp, methodology, match criteria, confidence category, and known caveats.
- Allow filters by relationship type, time range, provider/source, status, and confidence category.
- Allow navigation between a wallet, relevant transactions, and the public source that produced a match.
- Record evidence provenance and preserve it in saved cases and exports.
- Show contradictory or competing evidence when discovered rather than forcing one story.

I. EXPLAINABLE RISK/SUSPICION RANKING
Implement an inspectable, configurable, reproducible heuristic combining appropriate signals such as:
- Exact matches with trustworthy known indicators.
- Direct/short-hop relationship to the seed transaction/wallet.
- Temporal correlation.
- Amount similarity with documented fee/token adjustments where relevant.
- Graph structure and transaction behavior.
- Independent corroborating sources and known-entity labels.

Requirements:
- Display component values/contributions, supporting facts, missing inputs, and scoring limitations.
- Allow analysts to toggle signals and re-evaluate ranking.
- Keep the weights configurable and documented.
- Guard against double-counting copied reports or closely correlated signals.
- Do not call an arbitrary weighted score a probability. Unless calibrated and validated against suitable labelled data, call it a heuristic risk/suspicion score.
- Do not rank a wallet solely because it is new, active, near the seed, or connected to a bridge/exchange.
- Do not automatically identify a natural person. Separate “address labelled by source X,” “candidate relationship,” and “identity attribution supported by evidence.”

J. CROSS-CHAIN CANDIDATE TRACING
- Implement support for at least two EVM-compatible networks selected based on verified provider availability and access.
- Identify bridge contracts and mappings through official/documented sources where possible.
- Use bridge events, message identifiers, and documented correspondence for stronger deterministic linking when available.
- Where direct linkage is unavailable, generate clearly labelled candidate pairs using justified timing windows, token mapping, amount/fee adjustments, destination data, and additional verifiable features.
- Store the evidence for and against each candidate, method, time delta, amount comparison, and uncertainty.
- Label relationships as confirmed from documented events, candidate/inferred, or unresolved. Never present an uncertain match as a confirmed transfer.
- Surface privacy-mixer and attribution gaps honestly. Do not claim that every mixer or bridge can be traced.

K. ACTUAL PROGRESS AND PROVIDER HEALTH
- Use server-sent events or an equally appropriate mechanism to expose actual investigation progress.
- Report real stages such as acquisition, normalization, graph construction, OSINT enrichment, scoring, and report generation.
- Progress state must reflect completed backend work, not a fake timer.
- Show provider status, error/limit state, cache status, retrieval time, and partial-result warnings.
- Implement retry/cancellation behavior where appropriate and safe.

L. PERSISTENT INVESTIGATION CASEBOOK
- Create, save, reopen, rename, and delete cases using real persistent storage.
- Persist seed input, chain scope, time window, traversal limits, transaction data/source metadata as appropriate, findings, evidence provenance, rankings, relevant UI/graph state, analyst notes, bookmarks, review state, and timestamps.
- Allow wallet/edge bookmarking, notes, marking evidence reviewed, and recording investigative decisions.
- Reopening a case must restore its meaningful content; do not rely on in-memory frontend state.
- Provide sensible deletion/error handling and avoid storing API secrets.
- Preserve the ability to distinguish results that were observed live originally from cached or historical data.

M. HISTORICAL CASE AND INVESTIGATOR CHALLENGE MODE
- Include at least one genuine, labelled historical exploit/investigation case, using verifiable public transaction records and attributable source material.
- Document why the case dataset is authentic, how it was collected, its time range, and its known gaps.
- Run this dataset through the same normalization, graph, evidence, ranking, and reporting code as live investigations wherever practical.
- Create an interactive guided challenge: checkpoints, hints, transaction/wallet inspection, and evidence-supported questions.
- Check answers against the verified prepared case data and labels, not a hardcoded claim made by the live analysis engine.
- Clearly label historical/seeded records and keep them separate from live results.
- If the originally chosen incident cannot be verified or reliably acquired, choose another well-documented public case and report the reason. Do not fabricate an incident or its attribution.

N. REPORTS AND EXPORTS
Generate downloadable, functional reports, ideally HTML plus structured JSON and CSV exports where meaningful. Reports must include:
- Case metadata and seed.
- Scope/chains/time period and retrieval timestamps.
- Relevant transaction hashes, paths, addresses, assets, and explorer links.

- OSINT matches with source URLs, context, and source dates when available.
- Graph/temporal statistics and scoring explanation.
- Cross-chain links/candidates and their evidence category.
- Data provider, freshness/cache status, provenance, unresolved leads, and blind spots.
- Clear distinction between observed facts, reported claims, and algorithmic hypotheses.
- No unsupported claim that a particular person is the attacker.

Reports must be parseable/valid where appropriate, correctly encode values, avoid empty/fake result files, and exclude secrets.

O. PRODUCT-WIDE INTERACTIONS AND QUALITY
- Fully functional navigation, inputs, buttons, selects, filters, sorting, menus, drawers, modals, tooltips, search, graph controls, timeline, matrix cells, casebook operations, challenge actions, and export buttons.
